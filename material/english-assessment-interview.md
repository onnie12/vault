# English Assessment 1 – The Interview
**Interviewer:** Joe Steele
**Interviewee:** Adam Hilt, local guitarist
**Length:** approx. 4 minutes when spoken naturally
**Unit 1 vocabulary used is shown in bold.**

---

**JOE:** Hi everyone, welcome back to the show. Today I'm sitting here with a local musician. Adam, thank you very much for taking the time to talk to me today.

**ADAM:** Thanks for inviting me, Joe. I'm happy to be here.

**JOE:** So, could you tell me how you first got into playing guitar?

**ADAM:** Sure. It started on a school exchange trip to France. I was staying with a host family, and a family friend called Pierre played guitar every evening after dinner.

**JOE:** That sounds nice. Do you remember the first song he taught you?

**ADAM:** Yes, I do. It was "Come As You Are." He showed me the chords one by one, and I practised them all week.

**JOE:** Before music, did you have a normal job?

**ADAM:** I did, actually. I worked as an electrician for a small **firm** in town. I had a good **employer**, and the **income** was steady, but it wasn't really what I wanted to do with my life.

**JOE:** What made you decide to change that?

**ADAM:** Honestly, my **experience** with Pierre never really left me. After a few years I decided to **resign** from the job and try music instead.

**JOE:** That's a big step. Weren't you worried about the **qualifications** you needed, or about finding work as a musician?

**ADAM:** A little, yes. But I'm quite **strong-willed**, so once I decide something, I don't give up easily.

**JOE:** Did you **apply** for anything else, or did you go straight into music full-time?

**ADAM:** I took a **temporary** job for a while, just to be safe, and I played gigs on the side. Eventually there was enough work to go full-time.

**JOE:** How would you describe your personality? Are you more **laid-back**, or more organised?

**ADAM:** I'd say I'm pretty **easy-going**, actually. I don't get stressed easily. But on stage I can also be quite **strong-willed** about how a song should sound.

**JOE:** You don't come across as **self-centred** at all, if I can say so.

**ADAM:** [laughs] Thanks, I try not to be. I think being **open-minded** helps a lot in music, you have to listen to other musicians too.

**JOE:** Do you feel like you have a good **work–life balance** now, compared to your old job?

**ADAM:** Definitely. I feel more **well-balanced** now. I still work hard, but I enjoy every part of it.

**JOE:** What are you working on at the moment?

**ADAM:** At the moment, I'm writing some new songs for my next show.

**JOE:** Are you also performing anywhere this month?

**ADAM:** Yes, I am. I play every Friday night at a small café in town.

**JOE:** Your style reminds a lot of people of Eric Clapton. Would you say he's an influence on you?

**ADAM:** Definitely. I really admire his tone, and the way he takes his time with every note instead of rushing.

**JOE:** Could you tell me the names of a few of your own songs?

**ADAM:** Of course. "Copper Kettle Blues" is the most popular one. I also wrote "Six String Confession" and "Midnight on Rue de Lyon," which is about my trip to France.

**JOE:** Those are great titles. One last question: what keeps you motivated, even on difficult days?

**ADAM:** I always think back to the night I bought my first guitar. I try to feel that same excitement every time I play.

**JOE:** Adam, you must be glad you made that decision to resign, aren't you?

**ADAM:** [laughs] Yes, I really am.

**JOE:** Adam, thank you so much for talking to me today.

**ADAM:** Thank you, Joe. It was great being here.

*(End of interview.)*
